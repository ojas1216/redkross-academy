## Introduction and conceptual foundation

In a phishing case, containment is the work of limiting further harm while the organisation confirms scope and prepares recovery. A message can remain reachable in a mailbox, a device can still communicate outward, an authenticated session can remain active, and a payment can still be awaiting approval. Different controls address these paths. An analyst's task is to translate evidence into a bounded recommendation, give it to an authorised owner and verify that it achieved the intended effect. Treat each action as a change with a purpose, an approval path and an observable result.

The right starting point depends on what is at risk. A report of suspicious delivery without evidence of interaction may call for mail-side actions and further scoping. An employee reporting a secret disclosure may need identity response at once. Endpoint evidence of harmful activity may justify urgent isolation. The mail team's exact classification can continue in parallel. A response can be both fast and evidence-aware when the teams agree who is responsible for each risk and what evidence collection can happen without delaying needed protection.

## Separate the response tracks

**Mail and content.** Work with the mail owner to locate the original and related deliveries. Confirm the precision of the search on known matching and benign examples before asking for a wide quarantine or purge. A subject line may be common. A display name can match both malicious and legitimate mail. Prefer trusted message, campaign or trace identifiers where they are available, and include an explicit time window. The owner should record how many objects matched, how many were acted on and why any were excluded. Quarantine may preserve a recoverable copy and limit exposure, depending on the platform and policy. Deletion requires particular care if records need to be preserved.

**Identity.** Ask the identity responder to establish which account and services are in scope. A password change and a session action address different forms of access. The organisation may also need to inspect unauthorised methods, changed recovery information, suspicious application grants or mailbox settings. The effect of a given action depends on the service and token type. Confirm it with that service's owner and request an effective timestamp. When employee communications are in doubt, coordinate verification through an independently known channel. Record the protective actions and how the user will regain approved access when it is appropriate.

**Endpoint.** When a verified device shows relevant execution or suspicious follow-on activity, involve the endpoint responder. An approved isolation mechanism can limit communication while retaining evidence for further assessment. The owner must check what communication the isolation mode permits, whether the management channel remains available and whether the response can affect critical operations. An offline device may queue an action until it connects again. Reporting ?action sent? is therefore a different claim from ?isolation is effective.? Record the user impact and provide a known way for the employee to get assistance or a clean replacement.

**Network and business process.** A destination restriction can reduce further access when the indicator is precise enough and the owner can verify the policy effect. Consider whether the domain or service is shared by legitimate work and whether the policy covers the relevant route and clients. Keep logs enabled when policy permits so the SOC can review later attempts. Independently, a suspected alteration of payment details may require verification or a hold through Finance. The response owner should confirm which transactions or vendors were affected and what downstream decisions remain under review. A mail control cannot resolve a payment action that already happened.

## Preserve evidence while limiting harm

Name the artifacts the decision depends on. These may include the original message and trusted delivery trace, relevant authentication and session records, endpoint observations and case notes. Save them in the approved evidence store and preserve collection details. Where authorised staff can do so safely, collect state that an action would erase or change. If a harmful action is ongoing, the incident lead may direct immediate containment ahead of additional collection. Document the reasoning, observed effect and resulting evidence gaps. The decision must be intelligible later to a reviewer who was not in the room.

Avoid treating a generic request such as ?block the campaign? as approval for any action that could fit the phrase. Specify which assets, time range and identifiers the proposed action applies to. Include how it will be executed and who can approve deviations. The responder should know whether they may take a reversible action while waiting for further confirmation and who decides if the tradeoff changes. If a case has only a suspicious message and no observed transaction, say that. If an employee reports approving an unexpected prompt, state that promptly and distinguish the reporter's statement from provider logs.

A decision record can fit in a brief text entry. Include the observed risk, effective scope, responder, approving role, expected effect, verification and next review. For example, a case can request temporary quarantine of message identifiers matched to three confirmed deliveries, with one preserved specimen and a search rerun to find additional copies. It should say who evaluates unintended matches and how a legitimate message could be restored. The count is a fictional example, but the fields are practical. Replace the count with confirmed values in the actual case instead of copying a sample recommendation.

### Visual Walkthrough & Architectural Breakdown

The first step records a risk and its evidence. A mail delivery record, a reported credential entry and an endpoint process observation can each lead to a containment request, but they identify different assets and different effects. In the second step the incident lead identifies an owner and approves a bounded action. The plan says precisely which mail items, identities, devices or paths are in scope. It also identifies any evidence the action could change and any material operational cost. That preparation allows the team to choose a protective action without spending time debating ownership after it has triggered.

The third step combines evidence preservation with execution where circumstances permit. The responder records a request time and a result. The next step tests the effect from a source that can answer the question. If the mail team quarantines a set of messages, a disposition query can show which recipients are still exposed. If endpoint isolation was requested, look for the management result and review permitted endpoint and network observations. If the identity owner reports a session action, ask which services acknowledged it and what remains unknown. Do not ask a suspected user or a compromised system to perform a harmful action just to prove a block works. Use approved verification or safe test material.

The last step returns outstanding risks to the incident case. A control can succeed on two mailboxes and fail on a third, or a session action can complete while an unrelated account mechanism still needs review. The figure's loop sends that information back to the scoping step after recording the outcome. The owner can revise the plan when the evidence changes. For example, if the first search found two copies and a later message trace finds five, record the original decision's known scope and then approve the revised scope. This keeps the history auditable and gives the next shift a clear picture of effective protection and residual exposure.

## Worked example: the Banyan endpoint

The Banyan training dataset records an unusual download on WS-104 at 06:12Z and a suspicious Office child process at 06:14Z. Later records show unusual network activity and a 48.2 MB outbound transfer, whose contents are unknown. Those observations support prompt endpoint investigation and containment advice. In this dataset there is no original message or delivery trace, so it would be premature to list a campaign's recipients or claim a specific mailbox purge. The responding analyst should request authorised mail records from the owner if the scenario assumes they exist, and keep the endpoint case moving while that request is open.

A useful endpoint recommendation identifies WS-104 and references the process and network observations. Request isolation through the authorised endpoint owner under the incident lead's decision. Ask for an acknowledgment and timestamp, and confirm that continued collection is possible if needed. Capture available evidence according to the case procedure, without allowing optional collection to delay urgent protection. Include a plan for support to the legitimate user and for a clean route to essential duties. Make sure the report distinguishes the workstation's known signals from theories about the exact file content and the data in the transfer.

Separately, the training data records gopal's successful VPN authentication from an address associated with a burst of failures across users. A containment recommendation can ask the identity and VPN teams to review the session and act under the approved response plan. The data does not establish the endpoint used or show later activity inside that session. An inventory entry indicating gopal's usual workstation does not justify treating it as the observed client. Do not label the VPN and endpoint strands as one confirmed chain. The incident lead may coordinate the two responses while keeping the evidence and ownership distinct.

## Verify without making the case worse

Set a verification question before requesting each action. For mail, ask whether the correct objects were affected and how exceptions were handled. For endpoint, ask whether the targeted device reports the expected state and whether residual network evidence contradicts it. For identity, ask the owner to confirm which sessions and methods were acted on and to monitor for unexpected reappearance. For payment, ask Finance whether the identified case transaction is held or independently verified. A status field alone is insufficient when its definition is unclear. A completed request can still depend on a downstream system applying the change.

Use false-positive checks proportionate to the action. A proposed shared-domain restriction might affect many services, while a precise mail item quarantine may have a narrower cost. Start with the smallest scope that meaningfully reduces the identified risk when there is time and evidence to do so. Record why scope was expanded if related observations emerge. If the change causes an unrelated business outage, the incident lead needs a way to evaluate rollback, record the resulting risk and coordinate a safer control. A rollback is a decision with its own owner and timestamp, not an invisible erasure of the response.

Distinguish containment from recovery. A device may remain isolated for investigation. A user account may require a trusted re-enrolment and later monitoring. A transaction may remain held while Finance verifies it. Give each control a review owner and an end condition. If a closure criterion has not been met, retain the action and explain its operational impact. The case can move forward for other tracks. This discipline prevents an analyst from restoring an affected asset solely because the ticket has been open for a long time.

## Exercise: evaluate three recommendations

Review the following fictional requests as an incident lead. ?Delete all messages with invoice in the subject.? ?Confirm the targeted message IDs and preserve one specimen; quarantine matching deliveries and reconcile any failures.? ?Revoke the account's active sessions following the reported exposure and ask the identity owner to verify the set of affected services.? Identify the evidence each request assumes, its potential impact and what its owner needs to verify. Rewrite the first request so an operator can judge whether the proposed scope is justified.

Next, suppose isolation of WS-104 has been requested but the endpoint service says the device is offline. Write a status entry that records the request, the current verification gap and the next owner. The correct entry should distinguish between the observed offline state and the unconfirmed isolation effect. Identify which available source could help track later communication without overclaiming that silence is a result of the requested change. Then explain how your advice changes if the device comes online and the management system confirms the control. Compare the two responses against the original rationale.

Finally, suppose the identity team reports that its action is complete on a primary service but a separate application needs a different owner. Write one action per service and specify which is complete, which remains open and who will verify the rest. This activity trains the analyst to keep partial results visible. A case with uncertain scope can still have a precise statement about what was done and what was achieved.

## Coordinate dependent actions

Two owners may need to act within the same response window. An identity action may prompt a user to contact the help desk, while the endpoint owner needs the user to leave a device in its current state. Agree on the order, the communication owner and the conditions under which either team should act immediately. The incident record should be clear about which step depends on which confirmation. If one central approver is unavailable and the response plan authorises a backup, record the route that was used. When different teams work from inconsistent asset names, reconcile them using approved inventory or evidence identifiers before applying a change.

A handoff is especially important on a shift boundary. Give the incoming analyst the current action states, the outstanding verifications, and the time each owner will next report. Include any time-limited access restriction or quarantine so the analyst can check whether it expires automatically. A temporary policy may be justified during an investigation but should have a review and an owner before it is left in place. Store the result next to the other case decisions. If the next shift concludes that the case requires wider scope, the prior record lets them expand the response with a clear reason rather than restarting the analysis.

## Key takeaways

- Recommend mail, identity, endpoint and business controls according to the evidence for each exposure.
- State decision authority, scope, preservation needs and operational effects.
- Verify observable outcomes and record any remaining gaps separately from the request.
- Give temporary controls a review owner and a clear end condition.
