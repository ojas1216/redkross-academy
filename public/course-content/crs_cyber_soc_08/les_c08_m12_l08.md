## Shift-report objective

The Harbourline Finance capstone ends with a change of watch. Your report must let the incoming analyst take ownership without reconstructing the incident from thirty unconnected rows. Summarise each active or resolved case, the evidence supporting its current state, what changed during your shift, and what decisions still need owners. Preserve the distinction between a completed triage queue and a completed incident. The exercise is fictional and should be performed with the paper records and local test outputs only.

A good handoff makes two things easy. It shows the incident lead what could happen next and helps the analyst locate the actual evidence for each decision. A broad executive statement without provenance may suggest a decision that no one can audit. A full raw log transcript may be unusable under time pressure. Use a short top-level brief and a linked restricted worksheet or appendix. Both should share the same cutoff, meaning they describe what was known and verified at the same point in the simulated shift.

## Set the cutoff and account for the queue

Use 08:32 UTC on 2026-11-04 as the exercise reporting cutoff, after review of the second batch. State that all thirty supplied receipts are accounted for. A06 repeats A01 and B28 repeats B16. Record them as duplicate receipts with links to the underlying events. B27 occurred at 08:06 and arrived in the second batch, and is distinct evidence. Other rows include context, risk signals, operational status and data gaps. Do not describe the thirty rows as thirty harmful incidents or the twenty-eight non-repeat rows as twenty-eight confirmed compromises.

Review the worksheet by function. Each row needs a disposition and a linkage or owner. If an item supports a case, give the case ID and the evidence relationship. If it needs repair, give the responsible source owner. If it is routine, show the approved job or source supporting that disposition. If it is a duplicate, refer to the source event rather than deleting it. This accounting enables a later reviewer to verify what happened even if only two cases still require an urgent handoff.

State which outputs come from the detection exercises. The process-pair test and the identity test used separate synthetic rows to verify rule behaviour. Their printed results support claims about rule logic and test conditions. They do not supply additional operational evidence for the fictional case beyond the thirty paper records. For example, Lesson 7 expands a summary into fictional event details for a test; those details do not belong in the case as if the identity owner delivered a provider audit. A handoff should reference the test as a separate technical artifact and preserve where the case evidence came from.

## Present case decisions and residual risks

The FIN-21 case is the highest-priority endpoint thread. Evidence includes the document request, unusual endpoint process, subsequent DNS context and the observed outbound transfer. The control record reports acceptance pending at 08:27 and applied at 08:29 to the verified device. Report the effective control clearly, while keeping file content, data impact, delivery scope and payment questions open. The bounded proxy search found no second host in the checked records, time and three hostnames. Place its limits in the same sentence as the result.

The STAFF-382 thread is a distinct identity lead. Report the aggregate failure pattern and later observed success from the same source, with date, window and source. There is no confirmed client attribution and the provider's session information is unavailable to the analyst. Ask the authorised identity and VPN owners to verify legitimacy and any subsequent activity and to review appropriate protective options. State that linkage to FIN-21 is unconfirmed. A lead can protect identity in parallel with the endpoint investigation without claiming the cases are a confirmed chain.

The OPS-03 case can close for O-401 because CR-82 includes the asset, time and operation, the owner confirms the approved work and a forwarded archive is available for the checked interval. B28 remains a duplicate receipt for audit. The closure needs a re-open condition rather than a fleet-wide exception. Unexpected activity, an action outside the window, an unknown device or a missing required archive should be reviewed separately. Distinguish the scoped disposition from a proposal to change an underlying detector.

## Write the executive and analyst versions

An executive brief can be a few paragraphs. For example: ?An unusual process and outbound activity were observed on a payments workstation. The endpoint team confirmed isolation at 08:29 UTC. The content of the transfer, delivery and transaction impact remain under review. Separately, identity response is verifying an anomalous VPN success.? Add the named next decisions and the next update time. A further sentence can say that approved operations maintenance explained the reviewed OPS-03 event and no protective change was required for that case. Keep personal details and unrestricted raw records out of the executive version unless there is a need and authorisation.

The analyst version needs evidence links and current owners. Include the case identifier, UTC times, source references and conditions that count as completion. Put the release or notification status in its own entry. B23 is an incident-lead request to provide facts to the designated owner. It does not prove the SOC decided or sent an external notice. If the report says ?notification pending,? clarify whether facts have been assembled, approval is pending or transmission has been verified. The exercise supplies only the request, so the safest wording is that the handoff was requested and its outcome has not been established.

For FIN-21 identify which systems can provide the remaining evidence. Endpoint Operations can verify control and preserve evidence under the authorised plan. Mail can seek an original or trace if available. Finance owns transaction validation. The lead can correlate these updates but should not let one success close every case task. For STAFF-382 name the authorised provider or VPN owner. For collection gaps, hand off to the team that owns the relevant sensor or inventory mapping. Include next review conditions so those items do not grow stale.

## Set a clear next watch

Give each open item a time or event that triggers review. If the relevant team is unavailable, designate an approved fallback rather than leaving a general note that someone should act. For example, an incident lead may request an update after Finance reviews the transaction, and may schedule a shorter check for the pending identity scope. A training worksheet can use agreed exercise times. Label those proposed check times separately from occurrence and effective state. A scheduled update does not prove the action will be complete by then.

Ask the incoming analyst to acknowledge ownership in the exercise. They should be able to restate the most urgent issue, what is already verified, and what source would change the current assessment. Give them a route for corrections. A later source may amend a scope assumption or invalidate a prior classification. Add a new version of the shift brief with the reason rather than silently replacing the older note. This practice is valuable when two reviewers see the same incident at different points in its evolution.

Check the remaining open responses against the authority boundaries. The shift handoff does not authorise a protective action by itself unless the approved process explicitly records the decision. A pending action needs the case reference and requested approver. An observation of effective isolation has evidence for one workstation, not authority for a wider group. If a new asset is later observed, the lead can assess its risk and require fresh approval where appropriate. Keep urgency visible without transferring undefined authority.

## Reconcile technical follow-up

The first lab detector should identify one process-pair candidate, one repeat, two legitimate category negatives, one input-review record and one unsupported input for its exact fixture. The second detector should flag the illustrative eight-identity cluster and leave the one-identity control below threshold. The expected output of a local test can be checked independently. Preserve the test version, input and limitations, and name who would evaluate any production deployment. This is an opportunity to record technology follow-up without suggesting that the capstone code is running in the organisation.

Record a meaningful integration gap. The first detector needs verified endpoint schema and collection; the second needs trustworthy event identities and timestamp handling across late and multi-day events. Missing coverage means a negative output cannot guarantee absence. This context belongs in the shift record if it affects current scoping and in a separate engineering note if it concerns proposed monitor improvements. Keep the scope of an actual case separate from the quality of a proposed rule, even when both use the same scenario.

## Practical exercise and review

Write the final shift report with a cutoff, headline, case table, open actions with ownership, two important visibility limits and a reference to the evidence worksheet. Add a technical appendix summarising whether both local tests produced expected outputs. The appendix should say that their inputs are teaching fixtures. Ask a peer to trace each resulting claim to its source. If they identify a claim about unauthorised activity, payment changes or data content with no direct evidence, revise it to an appropriate assessment or gap.

Use four spot checks. Where is the record establishing applied endpoint status? What evidence justifies the specific operations closure? Who has authority to obtain the missing identity session data? Which written item indicates a request to pass facts to a notification owner and what does it actually prove? Then change the cutoff to 08:28 and revise the report as a second version. Endpoint isolation should be pending at that earlier point. The assessment of the process event still stands. Any records arriving after the change should not be recorded as known yet.

Finish with a case count explanation. The report records thirty supplied receipts, including two repeats, while the three case threads have distinct states at handoff. Other items are administrative observations and visibility questions. If your worksheet uses a different number of action items, explain how they group across owners and cases. The evaluator should be able to reproduce the intake count and see that every actionable risk has an assigned owner. A report is ready when it communicates status, authority, evidence limits and decisions without requiring the author to explain it orally.

## Test the handoff for operational use

Have a peer play the first fifteen minutes of the next watch. Give them the brief and ask which owner they would contact first, what evidence already verifies protection, and which action they would avoid repeating without a new decision. If a closure or pending item is ambiguous, note the precise field they needed. A handoff succeeds when the recipient can prioritise under realistic constraints, not merely when the author remembers why every line was written. Verify whether the next watch can find the approved contact and evidence reference without exposing material outside the case.

Build a final list of decisions that change the report later. An independently verified transaction change can alter the business impact. A trusted mail trace can establish more delivery scope. An authorised identity audit can resolve whether the observed session was expected and what resources it used. A newly observed unsolicited event outside scheduled maintenance can reopen a case. Give each decision a claim, relevant evidence and owner. This list narrows the next shift's task without demanding conclusions the available records cannot support.

In the capstone review, distinguish completion of process from completion of response. Every supplied receipt can have a disposition even when several actions are still waiting. Every lab detector can pass its fixed cases even though no live sensor integration was tested. A person who reports these distinctions clearly is demonstrating a measurable operational skill. Ask a peer to identify a closed case, an open case, a verified control and a pending data request in the report. Resolve any inconsistency between that result and the case worksheet before handoff.

## Key takeaways

- Account for the full queue while keeping events, effects and incidents distinct.
- Explain the evidence and limits behind each current case state.
- Record open risks with authorised owners and review times.
- Keep simulation and test results separate from the case evidence they illustrate.
