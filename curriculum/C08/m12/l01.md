## Capstone brief

You are the incoming analyst at Harbourline Finance, a fictional organisation. The handoff contains thirty training alerts and evidence records from one simulated shift. In this lesson you triage the first fifteen. The next lesson provides the remaining fifteen and asks you to reconcile the queue. Each case is fictional, and every observation is supplied as a paper record for analysis. Do not contact example destinations, open suspicious files, or operate a live account or service. Your job is to make defensible decisions from the records and to assign the next checks.

The assessment concerns triage, not whether you can guess a fictional attacker narrative. A queue contains actual security findings, routine activity, warnings when evidence is missing, and repeated records. An alert can justify an early protective handoff without establishing its final cause. A benign explanation needs supporting evidence. A duplicate should be linked so it does not vanish from accounting or inflate the number of affected systems. Write a disposition for every record and be prepared to defend it to the next shift.

## Operating context

The payments workstations are important because a disruption during settlement affects customer service. FIN-21 is assigned to payment review. OPS-03 belongs to the operations environment. WKS-05 is a training record for an office workstation. BAK-11 supports approved backups. These inventory facts give business context. They do not establish who created an observed process or which client authenticated. The organisation's endpoint, identity, mail and infrastructure responders own different systems. The incident lead can coordinate priorities and decide which changes to authorise.

The exercise opens on 2026-11-04 and all precise occurrence times are UTC. Events may arrive out of order. An item received later can describe an earlier occurrence. Preserve both times where both are provided. The displayed ordering is the intake order, so a later ID may support an earlier event. The initial view contains only the fields below. If a field is not present, write it as missing and ask for the source that could supply it. Do not fill it from knowledge you acquire in later capstone lessons without recording that it arrived later.

Use three work priorities. Urgent work has a plausible ongoing high impact or evidence strong enough that a responder should be engaged now. Review work has credible signals or unresolved scope and an owner who should check it during the shift. Routine or administrative work does not currently require a response action but still needs its disposition. You can start with another well-defined priority scheme if you map it to those meanings. Give a short rationale based on likely impact, evidence strength and urgency. A vendor severity is an input rather than the final answer.

## Build the decision worksheet

Give each alert a row with its ID, event time, observed facts and source, priority, disposition, owner and next verification. Use separate fields for linked case or item and confidence. You may use a local text file, an accessible spreadsheet or paper. Keep the IDs stable. A row that references an earlier one should say whether it is a duplicate delivery, additional evidence, or just a potential relation. For an urgent item, state the action you are requesting without saying it has happened. For a data gap, name the owner or expected source. If a timestamp is unknown, do not silently substitute the ticket creation time.

### Visual Walkthrough & Architectural Breakdown

Read from the source record down to the next action. The first step preserves the row's identity and the specific observations. It records a date and time or states that the time is unavailable. The second step separates a duplicate receipt from new evidence. A06 repeats the very same endpoint identity as A01. It should point to A01, with its own receipt accounted for. A02 names the same asset in a different source and at a different time. It is additional evidence rather than a repeat. This separation lets the reviewer count both the records handled and the unique signals correctly.

The third step asks what the evidence supports and what urgency follows. A file request by itself would justify context review. The combination of that request, an unusual process and later transfer on the same device may be enough to escalate promptly. The result should name the observations and the uncertainty about their exact causal relationship. A policy-bound action such as endpoint isolation is a decision for the authorised lead and responder. The queue can present a recommendation and an explanation of why accepting it would reduce risk.

The final steps give each item an owner and a verification question and prepare it for handoff. Missing data does not disappear on the review board. For the WKS-05 delay, the next owner checks when collection resumed and what interval remains unobserved. For the service desk screenshot, the mail reviewer can request the original and confirm disposition. No employee action can be inferred from an automated preview. A peer should be able to follow these steps without knowing the final scenario resolution. This method protects against prioritising solely by a product label or leaping from partial evidence to a fixed narrative.

## Work the urgent candidates

Start with A01, A02 and A15. They name FIN-21 but come from different observations. Construct a short timeline. Identify which record shows execution, which shows a requested file and which gives a later volume. Give the combination a priority appropriate to an important asset and potential ongoing impact. Record what you would ask the endpoint owner to verify. An example prompt is to confirm the process relationship and current device state through the source system, while the lead considers scoped protection. The proxy reviewer can search for comparable traffic across the available fleet. Mention the exact destinations and expected window in the restricted case note.

A03 is a relevant lead if it belongs to the same host and timeline. A11 is older, low-confidence context about the name in A03. Do not multiply confidence as if an external source and your own prior belief were independent confirmations. Think carefully about whether the observation supports a device owner search, a focused proxy query or a network control. A reviewer may be able to test whether the service is ordinary before escalating a broad restriction. Record why any recommendation is narrow and prioritised relative to the main endpoint finding.

A04 and A05 are a second thread. Many distinct identities failed from one address and the same address later authenticated as STAFF-382. That supports urgent or high-priority identity review depending on the policy and known business context. Ask for the actual client or session identifiers and evidence about subsequent activity, if collected. The record does not establish that one person controlled the account or that the VPN thread is related to FIN-21. Keep ownership with the VPN and identity responder and explain how the two investigations can proceed without deciding their relationship.

## Account for lower-priority and unresolved items

A08 has a matching approved job and is routine in the exercise unless conflicting evidence emerges. State the source of approval and preserve the reference. A14 accounts for a scheduled inventory reconciliation and does not itself verify the legitimacy of an endpoint process. A13 is a useful pointer to planned maintenance but lacks the full change record. If a matching operations alert appears later, verify its host, time, authorisation and actual change. A planned window can explain an observation only if the specific action is within scope.

A07 identifies a coverage gap. It does not establish that WKS-05 is compromised. It also makes silence in its endpoint log a weaker basis for ruling out activity. Assign a collection or telemetry owner and note the interval you cannot evaluate. A12 has poor provenance and needs data repair or independent identification. Keep its row visible. Do not correlate it to FIN-21 because it arrived near the other events. A reliable reviewer should be able to see both gaps without treating them as urgent security effects or throwing them away.

A09 is the reporter's statement and screenshot. Request the preserved original if available through the approved route and record there is no reported action. A10 documents an automated preview from a security service. The preview is a separate observation that can be linked to the report without being counted as an employee click. If the reporter later says they entered credentials or approved a prompt, escalate that action to the identity owner with time and source. Keep the earlier reported state and its later correction in the case history.

## Practical application

Fill every row, then summarise the queue in three short paragraphs. The first should identify urgent work and the requested decision for each thread. The second groups routine, duplicate and context records without hiding their references. The third names evidence gaps and their owners. Mark a clear cutoff and preserve which records were available by that point. This is a first-pass briefing, so do not write that an action is effective unless a source in the supplied batch establishes it.

Ask a peer to pick four rows at random. They should find the underlying record, reason for priority and next owner. Ask them to count one repeated delivery without counting a second endpoint signal. Ask which record supports an actual employee click and which only supports service activity. Ask them to identify an evidence gap that belongs with a telemetry owner. If they cannot answer from your worksheet, revise its fields rather than asking them to assume a relationship.

Try a comparison case. Move A15 later out of the initial batch so it arrives after the first case summary. The first report can still justify urgent endpoint review based on A01 and A02. The later transfer changes the impact question and might strengthen prioritisation. Record the update without rewriting the earlier snapshot as though the transfer was known at the time. That method lets a shift lead understand why earlier and later recommendations differ.

## Acceptance checklist

An adequate first pass accounts for fifteen identifiers, records A06 as a duplicate of A01, separates FIN-21 and STAFF-382 pending correlation, and leaves at least one clear evidence gap. The exact level assigned can differ if its rationale, owner and next check are adequate. A completion claim should be tied to the worksheet deliverable, not to final incident resolution. Note which checks depend on records you will receive in the next lesson. Carry both the scope and the uncertainty through that transition.

## Grade the first pass against observable decisions

Before the second batch, compare two reasonable ways to triage A01. An analyst might open an urgent endpoint lead immediately because a payments device shows a process relationship outside the expected context. Another might ask for a very short independent check of the category and target identity while keeping the item urgent. Both can be reasonable if their next action is bounded, evidence-backed and assigned to Endpoint Operations. Closing the item because the process log does not identify the document is weaker. The source establishes a concrete execution relationship and gives the response team a useful next check.

Apply the same reasoning to lower-confidence cases. A single historical reputation record has limited time and contextual value. Pair A11 with A03 and state that the records concern the same hostname, but do not treat the historical result as independent evidence that the observed DNS lookup was malicious. Give the owner a concrete comparison, such as approved service names and related activity in the checked proxy window. For A07, label the gap as delayed collection and record the alert source. The telemetry owner needs to know whether the delay is isolated, what interval has missing evidence and when a repeat check is due. Do not select a response action based on missing observations alone.

Make an exception list for claims that need stronger evidence. To state that the reporter clicked, ask for a reported action or appropriately attributed device or session evidence. To state the message actually reached a particular mailbox, ask for the trusted delivery record or an appropriately sourced original. To state that an account controlled a workstation, look for a corroborated client or session record rather than a shared clock range. This appendix need not be long. Its purpose is to stop a reviewer from lifting an inference into a situation report without its conditions.

Now compare anticipated ownership with the actual worksheet. Endpoint Operations can verify process and device status. Mail can preserve original message evidence and trace disposition. Identity and VPN owners can examine the authentication records. Finance can verify transaction history and ensure that a reported change is not mistaken for an approved payment. Infrastructure owns collection and inventory gaps. When a finding touches several groups, route separate work rather than leaving every field to one person. Ensure each reviewer has evidence references and can understand why their check is needed.

For an additional exercise, imagine the case system can show only one priority label per receipt. Retain the case's urgent label where justified and document the independent status of the supporting row. The original low-confidence source can support an urgent case while remaining low confidence. A duplicate can be linked to an urgent event without becoming a second urgent item. Compare how this improves the intake summary and the effectiveness of the handoff. A peer should be able to count work and explain which records changed urgency.

## Key takeaways

- Triage by documented evidence, potential impact and current urgency.
- Distinguish a repeated delivery from related new evidence.
- Route incomplete evidence with a specific next check and owner.
- Keep an accurate cutoff so later records can update the case without rewriting history.
